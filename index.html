The problem

Renting in Singapore still runs on WhatsApp photos, scattered PDFs and memory. When the tenancy ends, nobody can prove what the unit looked like at the start.

Deposit disputes

Move-out arguments over scratches, marks and fittings with no agreed starting record. Landlords lose money and tenants lose deposits unfairly.

Scattered admin

Inventory lists, meter readings, renewal dates and repair requests live in chat threads. Landlords who go direct (no agent) have no simple toolkit.

A rough landing for newcomers

New tenants, especially those arriving from overseas, must sort out movers, internet, electricity and cleaning without knowing who to trust. Many sign for a unit they have never seen.

Hidden duties for owners

Anyone renting out a unit or a room has approvals, stamp duty and tax to handle, spread across several agencies, and the rules differ for HDB and private homes.

Read the agreement first

CEA's HDB flat template has the tenant paying for minor repairs up to a per-item amount left blank for the parties to fill in, while the landlord keeps the structure, sanitary pipes and concealed wiring in repair. Air-conditioner breakdowns are the landlord's unless the tenant caused them. Your own agreement may differ.

Do not withhold rent or self-repair

The same template says rent is paid without deduction or set-off, and repairs above the agreed amount need the landlord's prior written consent. It gives no express right to repair and deduct. Rentnito would warn tenants before they take a step that could put them in breach.

Where it can end up

The Small Claims Tribunals can hear residential tenancy disputes where the tenancy does not exceed 2 years, for money claims up to S$20,000 (S$30,000 if both sides consent). Filing is S$10 up to S$5,000, S$20 up to S$10,000 and 1% of the claim above that. Mediation at a Community Mediation Centre may not cover repair neglect, so check first.

What Rentnito adds. A landlord who ignores repairs usually wins by silence, because the tenant has no record. The product gives tenants a dated paper trail and gives responsive landlords a way to show they acted. The reminder timings are product defaults, and the legal points come from CEA's template agreement and one legal information article. They are not legal advice, and a signed agreement overrides any template.

Dispute resolution: three tiers

Most deposit disputes are small and fact-based. Rentnito settles simple ones for a fee based on the amount in dispute, and refers everything else to established third parties.

Tier 1 · Free

Structured negotiation

Both sides see the same photos and an itemised claim, then trade offers within a fixed window. Included in the product and the main way most disputes should end.

Tier 2 · Percentage fee

Rentnito guided resolution

For simple cases that negotiation could not close. An AI-assisted proposal based on the evidence, reviewed by a person. Both parties opt in and the fee is shared.

Tier 3 · Third party

Referral

Anything contested, high value or sensitive goes to a Community Mediation Centre, Singapore Mediation Centre, a private mediator, or the Small Claims Tribunals.

How the percentage fee should work

Charge on the amount in dispute, not on who wins

A percentage of the disputed amount, with a minimum and a maximum, split between both parties and payable whatever the outcome. Taking a cut of what one side recovers would give Rentnito a reason to favour that side.

Keep neutrality visible

Landlords pay for the subscription, so a landlord-friendly bias is the obvious risk. Publish the decision rules, charge both parties equally, and let either side leave for Tier 3 at any time.

What counts as simple

  • One or two deduction items, such as cleaning or a wall mark.
  • Facts that the move-in and move-out photos can show.
  • Amounts below a starting cap (for example S$2,000) to be tested.

When to refer straight away

  • Facts that are not in the record.
  • Allegations of misconduct, safety issues or harassment.
  • Termination, possession or rent arrears questions.
  • Higher-value claims or a party who does not respond.

Third-party options (what I could verify)

Community Mediation Centre

Handles landlord and tenant matters including refund of a rental deposit, problems with the property and tenancy expenses, and small interest-free money matters under S$5,000. Its page does not state fees, so check with the Centre. It is not suited to legal, contractual or commercial disputes.

Singapore Mediation Centre

For disputes up to S$60,000 the schedule effective 1 March 2026 shows a S$27.25 filing fee and up to S$400 per party for a full-day session, GST included, with a surcharge on Saturdays. Larger disputes cost more.

Small Claims Tribunals

Covers residential tenancy disputes where the tenancy does not exceed two years. The claim limit is S$20,000, or S$30,000 if both parties sign a Memorandum of Consent. Lawyers are not allowed to represent parties. Filing fees were not stated on the page I read.

Mediation Act

A Ministry of Law note on the Mediation Bill says parties can have a mediated settlement recorded as a court order if they consent and the mediation was run by a designated provider or certified mediator. Community mediations are excluded. Confirm the final Act, because it matters for whether a Rentnito settlement is directly enforceable.

Still to check. Whether Rentnito's own tier could be treated as a regulated or legal service, whether any referral fee from private mediators would compromise neutrality, and PDPA handling of the photos and statements used in a dispute. Take Singapore legal advice.

Option: holding the rental deposit

Tenants usually pay about a month of rent as a deposit, so the money sitting in a deposit account could earn interest. It is a tempting extra revenue line, but it is also where the regulatory burden jumps. This section is kept out of the base forecast on purpose.

Bottom line. Holding other people's deposits probably needs MAS licensing, and investing them in securities probably needs more. The realistic route is to hold nothing at first, or to let a licensed bank or payment partner hold the money while Rentnito runs the process. I am not a lawyer, and the official pages I could read did not settle the escrow question, so get written Singapore legal advice before building anything that touches the money.

Path A

Do not hold deposits

Deposits stay between landlord and tenant. Rentnito provides the signed reports, the evidence pack and the dispute ladder. Lowest compliance and the right starting point.

Path B

Licensed partner holds the money

A licensed bank or payment institution holds the deposit. Rentnito runs the workflow and earns a fee or revenue share. Lower cost than a licence of your own, but the structure still needs legal advice.

Path C

Hold the money under your own licence

Most control and the most upside, with capital, safeguarding, audit and compliance costs. Only worth it at a scale this plan does not reach on Singapore alone.

Investing the deposits

Why I would not model it

  • The money belongs to the tenant or landlord and must be returned on time. Any investment loss would leave a gap that Rentnito would have to fill.
  • Managing customers' investments is itself a licensed activity under the Securities and Futures Act.
  • Payment institutions are expected to protect customer money. The MAS FAQ stops licensed e-money issuers from on-lending or investing customer funds, and I could not confirm what applies to other models.

The realistic version

  • Interest on a bank balance, held by a licensed partner, with the share paid to Rentnito set out in the contract and disclosed to users.
  • Possibly a fee per deposit instead of any interest, which is simpler and easier to explain.
  • No securities, no leverage, no use of the money for Rentnito's own costs.

Go-to-market, risks and compliance

Go-to-market (near zero budget)

  • Search content: how to get your deposit back, move-out checklist.
  • Reddit, Facebook landlord and tenant groups, HardwareZone.
  • Every shared report advertises the product to the other party.
  • Partnerships with small agents and inspection services later.

Key risks

  • Willingness to pay is untested. Mitigation: pre-sell before building much.
  • Cold start on the tenant side. Mitigation: free tier and link-based sharing.
  • Rently already runs a SingPass-verified, deposit-free rental platform in Singapore (launched January 2024). It is a fuller marketplace, so Rentnito must stay lighter and cheaper. A full competitor scan is still to do.
  • Deposit holding likely needs MAS licensing. Start with Path A or B.
  • Walkthroughs do not scale without hosts. Mitigation: test only after demand exists.

Compliance, kept light

  • Positioned as record keeping, not legal advice or agency.
  • PDPA applies to photos and personal data. Collect minimal data, no NRIC.
  • Tenant consent before any detail is shared with a partner (opt-in).
  • CEA guidance before listings or agreements.
  • Legal advice on MAS licensing before touching any deposit.
  • AI mediation presented as facilitation, never as legal advice.
